A digital handoff with autonomy

What to agree about access, documentation, dependencies and recovery so a project can keep moving after publication.

Publishing does not explain how to continue.

Handing over a website or application should answer straightforward questions: what you receive, what you can manage and who can help when something needs attention. A working screen does not answer all of them. We propose making continuity part of the scope conversation rather than introducing a checklist on the final day.

Before work begins, identify the materials to be delivered and the autonomy you need. They might include design files, a repository, publication instructions or a session to practise common tasks. Not every engagement needs the same package. A diagnosis does not automatically include implementing its recommendations, and a solution with external licences does not make those licences the client's property.

A useful result is an agreed inventory: deliverable, format, responsible person, acceptance criterion and exclusions. This guide offers questions for preparing that inventory. It does not independently extend the commitments of a proposal.

Separate account access from secrets.

Map the accounts that operate the project: domain, hosting, repository, editorial tools and integrations it actually uses. For each account, identify the administrator, who receives renewal notices and which permissions each participant needs. Access to an interface does not necessarily include the ability to recover an account or transfer its administration.

Documenting access does not mean pasting passwords into a manual. Credentials and service keys need an appropriate channel, limited permissions and a procedure for revocation or replacement. OWASP's reference expands on these secret management criteria. Do not share passwords or sensitive information in a commercial enquiry form, public screenshot or repository.

Include financial and operational dependencies too: subscriptions, third-party resources and maintenance responsibilities. The aim is not to eliminate every external provider. It is to understand what would happen if a tool changed or became unavailable, and who would evaluate the next step.

Test the documentation by completing a real task.

Useful documentation describes what the receiving person needs to do. An editor might update a product entry and review the result. A technical team might prepare the environment and reproduce a publication. Rather than measuring a handoff by its page count, agree on a few tasks that someone else can complete with the available instructions.

GitHub describes the README as a place to explain what a project does, how to get started and where to find help. In our proposed handoff, this reference is complemented by decisions, configuration requirements without secrets and known limitations. Instructions should match the delivered version rather than an earlier state that no longer works.

One straightforward check is to follow the procedure with the responsible person, note unclear steps and improve them. A session does not replace documentation, and documentation should not require everyone to become a developer. Each role needs a next step it can understand and take.

Clarify recovery and what happens next.

Agree on what is backed up, where it is kept and who can restore it. A repository backup protects the files it contains, but it is not automatically a backup of data, accounts or external services. GitHub documents different backup options and their scope. The operational question remains what this particular project needs to work again.

When recovery review is included in the scope, record the procedure, test conditions and limitations. Do not describe an isolated test as an availability guarantee. Likewise, a successful publication does not demonstrate that the product has achieved business goals. Keep technical evidence, observations from use and commercial metrics separate.

Close the handoff with identified outstanding work and clear responsibilities. Bug fixes, maintenance and new features may have different scopes. Being able to continue does not mean you will never need support. It means being able to choose that support with enough information, without missing access or documentation making the decision for you.
