From an ambitious idea to a verifiable scope.

Decide what to build, what to defer and how to recognize a useful delivery without confusing a feature list with a product.

Describe a need before selecting a solution

Ideas often arrive with a technology attached: an application, an automation or an AI assistant. Begin with the person who needs to accomplish something and what makes it difficult today. That description makes different solutions comparable, including the option of improving an existing tool.

The initial context does not need to be a perfect specification. An account of the current workflow, an example of friction and a real constraint can be more useful than a long feature list. Our aim is to reduce ambiguity without stopping learning.

Choose a complete, bounded journey

Building a small part of many features can produce a demonstration that nobody can actually use. Prefer one priority journey with an entry, a decision and a result. That makes it possible to evaluate missing information or an integration that does not respond.

Write down what is outside the scope too. Deferred does not mean forgotten. A decision record explains why a feature is absent, which assumption would need to change and which dependency prevents progress. It helps future work resume without repeating the entire conversation.

Turn adjectives into observable criteria

Fast, intuitive and modern are aspirations, not sufficient acceptance criteria. Translate each into something a reviewer can check. Examples include completing a task with a keyboard, preserving input after an error and loading a view without an optional visual dependency.

Not every check should be automated. Editorial review and physical-device tests may also be required. Identify what each piece of evidence demonstrates and what it leaves unverified. A favorable local result does not remove the limitations of its test environment.

Decide who retains control

Accounts, data, permissions and documentation need clear ownership. An integration that works but depends on an unknown personal account remains an operational weakness. Access, handoff and continuity are easier to discuss before they become urgent.

When automation is involved, define where it may act independently and what a person must confirm. This does not mean adding an approval screen to everything. It means distinguishing reversible actions from commitments affecting people, payments or important information.

Bring a useful signal to the conversation

Share what exists, what needs to change and the main blocker. Add examples that can be provided safely, without sending credentials or sensitive information through an initial contact channel. That is enough to begin discussing diagnosis and a sequence of deliverables.

This is an IzignaMx working framework, not a method that guarantees success. Priorities should change when evidence changes. For content pages, we also use Google’s questions about usefulness and people-first focus as editorial references rather than a formula for guaranteed rankings.
