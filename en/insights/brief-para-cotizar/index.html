What to share for a useful project proposal.

Prepare the context for your website or ecommerce, compare scopes and keep hidden assumptions out of the proposal.

Start with a task, not a page count

In an initial conversation, what a person should be able to accomplish is more useful than a list of screens. That might be comparing services, finding event information, requesting a consultation or buying. Describe what happens today and where uncertainty appears. A URL and one concrete example are enough to begin without writing a full technical specification.

Name the person performing the task and their context. Someone arriving through a QR code on the street may not have the same time as someone comparing providers at a desk. You do not need to prove everything before making contact. Separate known information from hypotheses so the proposal does not silently treat them as equivalent facts.

Separate launching, improving and evolving

A website may need a first version, a repair of its existing journey or an extension of its capabilities. These are different conversations. Make one short list of launch essentials and another of work that can wait. Include real external dates, such as an agreed campaign, and explain why they matter. Every preference does not need to become an urgent requirement.

Visual direction also needs context. Share references and explain what interests you: the reading experience, navigation, product presentation or an interaction. Asking for a modern appearance without its intended purpose leaves too much room for interpretation. Distinctive design can coexist with a direct route to contact.

Make dependencies visible

Identify content that is ready, required languages and who will review the copy. For a store, describe the approximate catalogue size and the tools involved in payments, inventory and delivery. Do not put passwords in a brief. Naming the accounts and confirming an authorized review process is sufficient at this stage.

Also explain who publishes changes after delivery. A team updating prices weekly needs a different workflow from a rarely updated information page. For forms, we review whether instructions, required fields and assistance are explicit, following W3C guidance. Visitors should not need to guess which information is missing or why the form will not continue.

Compare what each proposal includes

Two prices are not comparable if one includes content, migration and testing while the other covers only visual implementation. Ask about deliverables, exclusions, licences, review rounds and planned checks. Identify recurring costs and responsibilities that remain with your team. Indicative budget guidance can help prioritize without pretending to provide an automated quotation.

Finish with acceptance criteria: which journey will be tested, under which conditions and what documentation will be supplied. This is our working framework, not a sales guarantee. When budget is uncertain, start with the need and the constraints. A diagnostic stage may be a better first commitment than assuming that a complete rebuild is necessary.
